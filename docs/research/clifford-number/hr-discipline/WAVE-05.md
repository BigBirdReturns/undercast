# Clifford Number HR disciplinary-process Wave 05

## Counterpower depth, timing, and enforceability

Wave 04 defined counterpower as the ability to inspect, delay, alter, suspend, override, reverse, restore, or force revision. Wave 05 tests that definition against six named public instruments rather than treating every consultation, human signature, court order, or payment as equivalent.

The six instruments span three distinct timing classes:

```text
predeployment binding collective instruments
→ ZeniMax AI agreement
→ Blizzard QA collective agreement

predeployment soft participation instruments
→ Microsoft–AFL-CIO partnership
→ Microsoft–ACTU framework and Workers’ Summit

post-adverse-action challenge and remedy
→ Uber/Ola appellate information orders
→ Seattle deactivation procedure enforcement
```

## Central result

The public record recovers meaningful differences in power:

```text
dialogue ≠ consultation
consultation ≠ bargaining
bargaining ≠ preaction pause
explanation ≠ restoration
restoration ≠ system revision
system revision ≠ survivor-bias reconciliation
```

Two lanes recover binding collective instruments before a named harm. Two recover soft worker-input frameworks without deployment-specific decision power. Two recover post-harm external challenge or restoration. None publicly reaches a preaction hold, confirmed system revision, or continuing validation that includes workers who left, were silenced, overturned the classification, or were restored.

## Counterpower depth ladder

```text
0 no operative power recovered
1 information or dialogue
2 notice or consultation
3 bargaining or contractual rule
4 pause, hold, or preaction constraint
5 challenge, explanation, or review
6 restoration or individual remedy
7 system revision or binding change
8 outcome and survivor-bias reconciliation
```

The highest publicly recovered levels are:

```text
ZeniMax:        3
Blizzard QA:    3
Microsoft/AFL:  1
Microsoft/ACTU: 2
Uber/Ola:       5
Seattle:        6
```

A higher post-harm level is not automatically better than a lower pre-harm level. Timing remains part of the object. Seattle reaches restoration after deactivation; the ZeniMax and Blizzard instruments seek to shape rules before harm. The estate preserves both dimensions.

## Named boundaries

The ZeniMax public summary recovers a legally binding agreement, notice of AI implementation that may affect represented work, and bargaining over impacts on request. It does not disclose the complete clause, a general veto, automatic pause, worker-level explanation, grievance remedy, or system-revision result.

The Blizzard public summary recovers a ratified contract with enhanced AI and generative-AI regulations plus reasonable disability-accommodation protections. The public record does not disclose the complete contract text or a named deployment outcome.

The Microsoft–AFL-CIO partnership recovers education, worker input into technology development, policy collaboration, and an organizing-neutrality framework. It is not treated as a deployment-specific collective agreement.

The Microsoft–ACTU framework recovers a statement of shared intent, good-faith consultation if disputes arise, and an actual Workers’ Summit. It does not establish a binding deployment veto or remedy.

The Uber/Ola appellate record recovers worker challenge and a judicial information order after automated account-removal decisions. Evidence access is not presumed to be restoration or model correction.

Seattle recovers procedure rights and public resolutions involving process restarts, reinstatement or reactivation, and payments. Through 31 May 2027 the public enforcement boundary remains procedure-focused; these records are not promoted to merits findings about the underlying deactivation reason.

## Chloe Moffat boundary

Chloe remains a named non-AI baseline. The comparison is limited to counterpower timing, notice, support, independent welfare, distress stops, and accountability routing. No AI use, automated decision, vendor system, final coroner conclusion, liability finding, culpability finding, or victim-character inference is permitted.

The prevention gap is nevertheless visible: the public record recovers family advocacy, inquest scrutiny, and reported procedure changes only after irreversible harm. Wave 05 does not convert that sequence into a final causal finding. It preserves the institutional timing problem.
