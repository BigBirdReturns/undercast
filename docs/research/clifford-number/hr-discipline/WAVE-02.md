# Clifford Number HR disciplinary-process Wave 02

Wave 01 recovered a named non-AI adverse-process sequence: an unexpected allegation meeting, practical formal-threat signals, denied support, care-control collision, acute distress, and unresolved public accountability. Wave 02 does **not** rewrite that case as an AI event. It treats it as a baseline for testing how algorithmic management can accelerate the same failure surfaces.

```text
worker exhaust captured
→ institution authors the norm
→ deviation, tone, risk, pace, or sentiment is scored
→ alert enters HR, security, compliance, or management queue
→ work context is compressed
→ a human ratifies under automation and workload pressure
→ access, pay, duties, reputation, account, or employment narrows
→ workers conform, self-censor, avoid scrutiny, or leave
→ the survivor population becomes the next baseline
→ dissonance suppression can be mistaken for fit, safety, or engagement
```

The estate records the user's phrase **AI purge of dissonance** as a bounded control-loop hypothesis, not a universal factual conclusion. The operative term is **purge-capable algorithmic dissonance suppression**: a system can make disagreement and deviation easier to classify and remove without any single actor declaring an intent to purge dissent.

## What is already industrialized

The OECD employer survey defines algorithmic management broadly enough to cover scheduling, task allocation, time, speed, conversation content or tone, location, fatigue, safety, targets, rewards, sanctions, and leaderboards. It reports at least one instruction, monitoring, or evaluation tool at 90% of surveyed US firms and an average 79% of firms in the surveyed European countries. The packet retains the essential limitation: not all these tools are AI-powered, and broad adoption does not establish punitive use in a named deployment.

Electronic performance monitoring cannot be presumed beneficial merely because it produces more data. The recovered 94-sample meta-analysis found no evidence of improved performance and found higher worker stress. EU-OSHA reports associations among automated allocation or monitoring, severe time pressure, overload, isolation, reduced autonomy, and stress, depression, or anxiety. Those are population-level findings, not a diagnosis or case-specific causal conclusion.

## Dissonance suppression without a declared purge

The control loop does not require an explicit field called `dissent`. A system may score tone, sentiment, risk, compliance, productivity, time off task, deviation, engagement, or anomaly. If the worker's context arrives only after the score has triggered restriction or scrutiny, the output has already shaped the burden of proof.

Research recovered in this wave distinguishes algorithmic functions. In one matched field study, directing, scheduling, and monitoring inhibited constructive voice, while algorithmic feedback promoted it. The estate therefore refuses the claim that automation is uniformly suppressive. It instead asks which function the system performs and what role signal it sends: passive execution, or supported improvement.

The survivor-conditioned hazard appears later. Workers who self-censor, exit, are deactivated, or lose opportunity cease producing the future labels used to evaluate the policy. Lower complaint volume or lower behavioral variance can then be read as organizational health even when the system has merely removed or chilled the people most likely to contest it.

## Chloe is the non-AI baseline

The parent record contains no public evidence that AI or an automated system was used in Chloe Moffat's process. Wave 02 therefore permits only a mechanism join:

- automated complaint intake could harden an uncertain allegation faster;
- risk scoring could turn a preliminary concern into an apparently objective severity label;
- workflow automation could preserve an informal label while triggering access and communication restrictions;
- wellbeing analytics could add another score while leaving independent human support absent;
- generated summaries and recommendations could turn a nominal reviewer into a ratifier;
- later training on surviving cases could reproduce the original omission because silent, exited, or harmed workers remain outside the validation set.

None of those propositions says that they occurred in Chloe's case.

## Named metric and vendor surfaces

The court record in *Moses v. Amazon* describes a computer-generated 205-minute time-off-task report followed by a manager and HR business partner meeting. The packet uses it only as one public metric-to-process chain. It does not generalize the exact system, policy, or legal result across Amazon.

California AB 701 shows that quota description, personal work-speed data, adverse action, breaks, safety, and retaliation can be made separately addressable. The EU Platform Work Directive adds a more complete future-facing platform transaction: human decisions for termination-equivalent detriment, reasons, competent review, rectification, recurring impact evaluation, health-risk controls, and retained platform duty even when an external provider operates the system.

Vendor materials show the available classification surface. Mimecast Aware advertises sentiment, topic, toxic-behavior, employee-pattern, and risk analysis across collaboration data. Workday Peakon advertises AI summaries across more than one billion aggregated responses and 200 million written feedback items, including workload and burnout themes. Those are capabilities, not misuse findings. Microsoft Viva's individual-only insights and differential privacy provide a counterexample showing that privacy-preserving design and declared non-evaluation boundaries are possible.

- the CFPB worker-surveillance release is archived, and the Seattle appellate record is a preliminary-injunction ruling rather than final resolution of every merits question.

No one legal object supplies the complete adverse-AI transaction.
